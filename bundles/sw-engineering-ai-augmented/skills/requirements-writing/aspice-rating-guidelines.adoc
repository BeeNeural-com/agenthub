= SWE.1 — Software Requirements Analysis: ASPICE Rating Guidelines
:source: VDA Automotive SPICE Guidelines, 2nd Edition 2023, Section 3.8
:toc: left
:toclevels: 3

== Purpose

Establish a structured and analyzed set of software requirements consistent with the system requirements and system architecture, that is a basis for software design, software verification, and software integration test.

== General Interpretation Guidance

=== Iterative vs Incremental Development

Requirements do not need to be complete at the project start. Two common patterns:

* *Iterative development* — existing requirements are refined over several iterations.
* *Incremental development* — new requirements are added in each cycle.

Both are valid; the assessor evaluates the consistency and traceability of the requirements _as they exist at assessment time_, not their completeness relative to a final vision.

=== What BP1 Covers (Software Requirements the SW Shall Implement)

BP1 specifies the software requirements that the software component shall implement. This includes functional and non-functional behavior visible at the software boundary.

=== What BP4 Covers (Impact on Operating Environment)

BP4 captures the impact of the software on its operating environment, including:

* Human users and HMI ergonomics / fatigue effects.
* Target hardware (I/O behavior, resource usage).
* External systems the software interacts with.

=== Requirements Structuring and Categorization

Requirements may be categorized in multiple ways (functional/non-functional, priority, release assignment). If only a coarse "functional/non-functional" split is used — and no finer decomposition into structural categories (e.g., state, data, interface, constraint) — this affects SWE.2 (see RL.4).

=== Requirements Analysis (BP3)

Analysis includes:

* Technical feasibility assessment.
* Impact on project scope, schedule, and budget.
* Identification of conflicts and dependencies.

Evidence does not have to be a formal report — tool attributes, comments, or review records are sufficient (see RL.7 and RL.8).

=== Traceability (BP5)

Traceability between software requirements and their upstream origin (either SYS.2 software-relevant system requirements or SYS.3 software architectural design, or both, depending on the project). Only one upstream path needs to be maintained — not both simultaneously (see RL.10).

== Rating Rules Within the Process

=== RL.1 — SW-Only Development: Traceability to Stakeholder Requirements

*Condition:* The project performs software-only development (no separate SYS.2/SYS.3 process) and traceability between software requirements and stakeholder requirements (SYS.1) has been established.

*Consequence:* `SWE.1.BP5` shall *not* be downrated.

=== RL.2 — Platform / Product-Line Requirements as Input

*Condition:* Software requirements originate from a platform or product-line specification (not from system requirements of the current project), and those requirements are consistent and non-contradictory with the project context.

*Consequence:* `SWE.1.BP1` shall *not* be downrated.

=== RL.3 — SW Requirements Derived Directly from Stakeholder Requirements

*Condition:* Software requirements are derived directly from stakeholder requirements without an intermediate system requirements (SYS.2) step, and this approach has been agreed with both software and systems representatives.

*Consequence:* `SWE.1.BP1` shall *not* be downrated.

=== RL.4 — Only "Functional / Non-Functional" Categorization Used

*Condition:* The only requirements categorization used is the coarse "functional" / "non-functional" split, with no finer structural breakdown.

*Consequence:* `SWE.2.BP2` shall be rated *N* (not applicable / not satisfied) because finer categorization is a prerequisite for proper architectural allocation.

NOTE: This rule impacts _SWE.2_, not SWE.1 itself — it is captured here to alert requirements authors that their categorization choices have downstream effects.

=== RL.5 — Release Plan as Prioritization Evidence

*Condition:* The only evidence for requirement prioritization is the project release plan (requirements are assigned to releases).

*Consequence:* `SWE.1.BP2` shall *not* be downrated. A release plan is a valid, sufficient form of prioritization evidence.

=== RL.6 — Requirement-to-Release Mapping Inconsistent with System Requirements

*Condition:* The mapping of software requirements to releases does not align with the priority or release assignment of the corresponding system requirements.

*Consequence:* `SWE.1.BP2` shall be *downrated*. Release alignment must be consistent up the traceability chain.

=== RL.7 — Tool Attributes as Analysis Evidence

*Condition:* Analysis results (e.g., technical feasibility judgements) are captured as tool attributes, review comments, or inline annotations rather than as separate analysis reports.

*Consequence:* `SWE.1.BP3` shall *not* be downrated. The form of the record does not matter; what matters is that the analysis was performed and recorded.

=== RL.8 — Technical Feasibility Covered by Risk Management

*Condition:* Technical feasibility analysis is captured in the project's risk management records (e.g., as identified technical risks with mitigations) rather than as a standalone analysis artifact.

*Consequence:* `SWE.1.BP3` shall *not* be downrated.

=== RL.9 — Analysis Results Not Consistently Used by Project Management

*Condition:* Although analysis results exist, project management does not consistently act on them (e.g., schedule or scope are not adjusted in response to identified feasibility issues).

*Consequence:* `SWE.1.BP3` shall *not* be downrated at the SWE.1 process level. The failure to act on results is a project management process issue (MAN.3), not an SWE.1 non-conformity.

=== RL.10 — Only One Traceability Path Maintained

*Condition:* Traceability exists either to SYS.2 (system requirements) _or_ to SYS.3 (system architecture), but not to both.

*Consequence:* `SWE.1.BP5` shall *not* be downrated. Maintaining two redundant traceability paths to both SYS.2 and SYS.3 is not required — one coherent path is sufficient.

== Rating Rules With Other Processes at Level 1

None defined for SWE.1 in the VDA Guidelines.

== Key Base Practices Summary

[cols="1,4", options="header"]
|===
| BP | Subject
| BP1 | Specify software requirements — what the SW shall implement and its impact on the operating environment.
| BP2 | Structure and prioritize software requirements — categorize, assign to releases or components.
| BP3 | Analyze software requirements — feasibility, impact on schedule/budget, conflicts.
| BP4 | Analyze the impact on the operating environment — hardware, users, external systems.
| BP5 | Establish bidirectional traceability — between SW requirements and their upstream origin (SYS.2 or SYS.3).
|===
